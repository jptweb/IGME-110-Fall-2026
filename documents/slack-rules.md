# IGME-110 Slack Ground Rules

I have never had official rules in a class Slack before, and I still want this to be a relaxed
place. These exist to set some expectations and to give you a better idea of how to actually use
Slack for this class.

> **AI notice:** I had a conversation with Claude to develop these guidelines. See rule 5.

This workspace is part of the course, so everything in it falls under the RIT Student Code of
Conduct the same way the classroom does.

Read these once and react with 👍 on the pinned post in **#110-announcements**. That is part of
the week 1 activity.

## 1. Be decent to each other

Disagree with ideas as much as you want. Do not go after people.

This class runs on critique, and critique only works if the room feels safe enough that people
will put unfinished work in it. If something here makes you uncomfortable, message me directly
and I will deal with it.

## 2. Ask course questions in public

Post in **#110-general** instead of DMing me. Someone else almost certainly has the same
question, and you will usually get a faster answer, because the TAs and the other 110 instructor
watch that channel too.

Save DMs for things that are genuinely private: grades, personal situations, an emergency.
Extensions have their own channel, see rule 8.

## 3. Reply in threads

Use **Reply in thread** instead of posting into the main channel. It keeps a channel of 60-plus
people readable. If you break a thread out into the channel, someone will probably tease you
about it.

## 4. Help each other, but do not hand over your work

Helping is expected: explain a concept in your own words, point someone at the documentation or
the setting they missed, sit with their problem and talk it through, tell them what fixed it for
you.

Handing over your finished file, document, or design so someone can copy it is not helping, and
neither is doing part of their assignment.

**The test: after you help, could they do it again on their own?** If yes, you helped. If no,
you did it for them.

Inside your own project team none of this applies. Share everything with your team.

## 5. Disclose AI here too

Course policy is that AI tools are fine unless an assignment says otherwise, as long as you say
which tool you used and what you used it for. That applies to answers you post in Slack.

If you paste something an AI wrote, say so. An unmarked AI answer that turns out to be wrong
costs someone an hour, which is worse than not answering at all. As an example, see the notice at
the top of this page.

## 6. Other people's work stays where they put it

Screenshots of a classmate's project, critique comments written about them, or anything from a
private team channel do not get reposted without that person's okay.

## 7. Ask questions that can actually be answered

"It's not working" cannot be answered. Say what you were trying to do, what happened instead,
and add a screenshot if there is anything to see. You will get help much faster, and you are
practicing something you will do professionally for the rest of your career.

## 8. I am not on Slack at 3 AM

Message me whenever you want. Just do not expect a reply outside working hours, though your odds
are much better here than over email. I read Slack on weekdays and usually reply the same day.

**Extensions do not go through DM.** Go to **#110-extension-request** and use the workflow
embedded in the channel. Ask at least 12 hours ahead and I will usually say yes. Ask after the
deadline and the answer is usually no.

If something is genuinely going wrong in your life, DM me as early as you can instead. Things
come up. It just has to be a conversation before the deadline rather than after it.

## 9. Channels

- **#110-announcements** for things from me. Read-only for you.
- **#110-general** for questions about any part of the class.
- **#110-inclass-activities** for posting your work from in-class activities.
- **#110-random** for everything else.
- **#110-introductions** for your intro post in week 1.
- **#110-extension-request** for extension requests. The workflow is in the channel.
- **Your team channel** for project coordination, added later in the semester.

Every channel in our workspace starts with `110`, so they group together in your sidebar.

Signing in, setting up your profile, and tuning your notifications are all covered in the week 1
activity, [Using Our Slack Workspace](../activities/slack-workspace.md).
