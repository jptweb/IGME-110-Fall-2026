# IGME-110 Slack Ground Rules

This workspace is part of the course, so everything in it falls under the RIT Student Code of
Conduct the same way the classroom does.

Read these once and react with 👍 on the pinned post in **#110-announcements**. That is part of
the week 1 activity.

## 1. Be decent to each other

Disagree with ideas as much as you want. Do not go after people.

This class runs on critique, and critique only works if the room feels safe enough that people
will put unfinished work in it. No harassment, no slurs, no "it was a joke." If something here
makes you uncomfortable, message me directly and I will deal with it.

## 2. Ask course questions in public

Post in **#110-general** rather than sending me a DM. Someone else almost certainly has the same
question, and you will usually get a faster answer, because the TAs and the other 110 instructor
watch that channel too.

Save DMs for things that are genuinely private: grades, extensions, personal situations.

## 3. Reply in threads

Use **Reply in thread** instead of posting into the main channel. It keeps a channel of 60-plus
people readable. If you break a thread out into the channel, someone will probably tease you
about it.

## 4. Helping each other is encouraged. Handing over your work is not.

You are allowed and expected to help each other. That looks like:

- Explaining a concept in your own words
- Pointing someone at documentation, a tutorial, or the setting they missed
- Sitting with someone's problem and talking through it
- "I hit that too, here is what fixed it"

What crosses the line:

- Posting your finished file, document, or design for someone else to copy
- Doing part of someone's assignment for them
- Sharing your work with someone who then turns in something recognizably the same

**The test: after you help, could they do it again on their own?** If yes, you helped. If no, you
did it for them.

Inside your own project team this does not apply. Share everything with your team.

## 5. Disclose AI here too

Course policy is that AI tools are fine unless an assignment says otherwise, as long as you
disclose it and include a transcript. That applies to answers you post in Slack.

If you paste something an AI wrote, say so. An unmarked AI answer that turns out to be wrong
costs someone an hour, which is worse than not answering at all.

## 6. Other people's work stays where they put it

Screenshots of a classmate's project, critique comments written about them, or anything from a
private team channel do not get reposted without that person's okay.

## 7. Ask questions that can actually be answered

"It's not working" cannot be answered. Include what you were trying to do, what happened
instead, and a screenshot if there is anything to see.

You will get help much faster, and you are practicing a thing you will do professionally for the
rest of your career.

## 8. I am not on Slack at 3 AM

I read messages during normal working hours on weekdays and will usually reply the same day. Do
not build a plan that depends on an instant answer the night before a deadline.

Extensions run through Slack the same way: message me **at least 12 hours before** something is
due and I will usually say yes. Ask after the deadline and the answer is usually no.

## 9. Channels

- **#110-announcements** for things from me. Read-only for you.
- **#110-general** for questions about any part of the class.
- **#110-random** for everything else.
- **#110-introductions** for your intro post in week 1.
- **Your team channel** for project coordination.

Every channel in our workspace starts with `110`, so they group together in your sidebar.

## 10. Keep your profile recognizable

Add a profile picture so we can learn who you are. A photo is best, an avatar is fine.

Your display name comes from the RIT directory and cannot be changed inside Slack. To change it,
go to start.rit.edu, then Directory Information, then Name Preferences. That updates Slack, Zoom,
myCourses, and class rosters together.

Do not impersonate anyone.
