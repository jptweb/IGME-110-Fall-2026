## IV. Brainstorming a Product Idea

Next week you'll brainstorm app ideas with your project team. Today you'll do it alone, with a
**thinking partner** rather than an answer machine. For most of you that partner is AI. There's
a version that uses a classmate instead, and it earns the same credit.

There's a real difference between thinking *with* something and being handed an answer, and
learning it now will save you a lot of embarrassment later.

> **Heads up on logins**, since you'll do two in one class. Slack is your RIT username with no
> domain. Gemini is your RIT **Google** account, `abc1234@g.rit.edu`. Different systems,
> different formats, and mixing them up is the single most common way to get stuck today.

**Pick one problem:**

- Doomscrolling
- Brain rot
- Group project scheduling

**→ [Full instructions: Brainstorming a Product Idea](../activities/brainstorming-activity.md)**

Open it and follow along. It has all the prompts and steps. You post your idea to Slack before
you leave, then paste the link to it in the myCourses dropbox.

Remember the course AI policy: **using AI is fine, hiding it is not.** Part of what you post
today is one thing the tool got wrong or generic, which is the habit worth building.

**If you'd rather not use AI,** the instructions include a partner version that earns the same
credit. Take it if you want it, no explanation needed.

## V. Project 1: App Proposal

Your first project is a **researched proposal for a product**. It could be a phone app, a
website, a desktop app, or a physical product.

Your group researches the idea thoroughly and makes a case compelling enough that someone
would want to fund it.

**Teams get assigned next week**, so there's nothing to do about that today. This is just so
you know what's coming.

The thing that makes this project work isn't the idea. It's whether you can answer *"why do
you think this is worth building?"* with something better than a hunch. That skill is most of
what this course is about.
