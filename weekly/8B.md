## I. Push or Pull?

We started with doors. Most of them had a sign saying PUSH, and most of you still wanted to pull,
because the handle said pull louder than the sign said push. When the sign and the design disagree,
people trust the design.

The flat crash bar is the opposite: nobody gets it wrong, and it doesn't need a sign. The foot pull
on the latching bathroom door in Orange Hall is the funny one. It asks you to use your foot, but you
have to grab the handle to unlatch it anyway.

> "The design of the door should indicate how to work it without any need for signs, certainly
> without any need for trial and error."
>
> Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things*

You'll go much deeper with that book in IGME-236, and the RIT library has a digital copy if you want
to start early.

## II. Games Have the Same Problem

Games mark climbable ledges and ladders with **yellow paint** so players don't get stuck. Some
players love it and some find it insulting. *Marvel's Wolverine* shipped with a scent trail to the
objective that players made fun of until it got toned down in a patch.

> "Get rid of it and some games become unplayable for folks. Keep it and people will make fun of it
> and complain."
>
> Zack Zwiezen, Kotaku, 2024

Apps do this too, with onboarding tours that dim the screen and point at buttons. Most of us skip
them. *Super Mario Bros.* World 1-1 (1985) has no text and no tutorial: the first enemy walks
straight at you, and that teaches you to jump.

**How do designers decide? They watch someone use it.** If testers walk past the ladder, you need
the paint, or a better ladder. That's what Project 3 is about.

## III. Where This Course Goes Next

One product idea, four projects: Project 1 found a real problem, Project 2 explains it at a glance,
Project 3 makes it clickable and puts it in front of people, and Project 4 sells it.

We looked at five design tools. Here's which ones Project 3 asks you to make:

| Tool | What it is | In Project 3? |
|---|---|---|
| **Personas** | A made-up person, described like a real one | Yes, one per teammate |
| **Sitemaps** | Which pages exist and how they connect | No, just know what one is |
| **Wireframes** | Where things go, not how they look | Yes, your lo-fi prototype |
| **Mockups** | How it looks | Not separately. The look goes into your final version. |
| **Prototypes** | Something you can click | Yes, twice: a paper one, then one in Figma |

The full brief is on the [Project 3 page](../documents/p3-prototype.md). We start on Tuesday.
