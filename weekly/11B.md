# Week 11B: Test Day

**Thursday, November 5**

> **Today in one line:** Another team tries your lo-fi prototype while you watch, then you swap
> **Bring:** your paper screens. I'll have your scripts printed.

## In class

Sit with your [partner team](../documents/p3-teams.md). The team listed first has its prototype tested first.

1. **First team's prototype:** at least two testers, one at a time, then a short debrief
2. **Swap**
3. **Second team's prototype:** the same thing

Use a new copy of the script for each tester. If you finish early, run a third tester. In Section 01,
Teams 8 and 9 test each other at the same time.

## The rules

- **Don't help.** "What would you do here?" is the only hint. When a tester gets stuck, the test is
  working, so write down where.
- **Testers think out loud.** If one goes quiet, ask "What are you thinking?"
- **Not your turn? Turn around.** At the very least, don't look at the papers. If a tester has seen
  your screens, mark that at the top of the script.

## Debrief

The Scribe reads back where testers got stuck, the testers say what the crew missed, and then the
team writes down three possible fixes.

## Friday night

Take photos of your filled-in scripts before you leave. Then hand in
[one PDF per team](../documents/p3-prototype.md#3-testing-results) on myCourses:

1. A one-page summary: what happened on each task, any patterns, and three fixes
2. Every filled-in script, from at least two testers
3. Your AI line at the end

Each fix has to point to something a tester did. "Two of our three testers looked for Save at the top
of the screen, so we're moving it there" is a fix. "Make it more intuitive" isn't.

Only got one tester today? Find another before the deadline. A roommate or friend who hasn't seen your
app counts.
