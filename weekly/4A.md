## III. Copyright, the Short Version

A quick pass through how copyright works, then the things that matter most when you go looking for
assets.

| | |
|---|---|
| **If someone made it, assume it's copyrighted** | No © symbol or registration needed. It lasts the creator's life plus 70 years. In the US, anything published more than 95 years ago is public domain. |
| **Visible on the internet is not public domain** | This is the most common mistake by a wide margin. Being able to see something and right-click it has nothing to do with whether you may use it. |
| **Fair use is a defense, not a permission** | It is what you argue after you have been sued. Nobody can tell you in advance that something is definitely fair use, and "it's for school" is not an automatic pass. |
| **Creative Commons is a family of licenses, not one license** | BY, BY-SA, BY-NC, BY-ND, CC0. Two of those letters matter most to you: NC means non-commercial, and ND means you may not change it. |
| **Music in a video needs two permissions** | One for the song and one for the recording. Project 4 is a video ad, and this is where most people get caught. |
| **Copyright and plagiarism are different problems** | Copyright is whether you're allowed to use it. Plagiarism is whether you gave credit. The scavenger hunt asks for both. |

Something good to know: at RIT, the work you make for your classes is generally yours. A job or
co-op can be different, so read the agreement before you sign it.

**The practical version of all of it:** when you find an asset, the question is never "does this
look free." It's "where is the license, and what does it say I have to do."

## IV. The Scavenger Hunt

**[→ Activity: Asset Scavenger Hunt](../activities/asset-scavenger-hunt.md)** · 10 points · due
tonight

Six items. For each one you find a legally usable asset and record four things: the asset, the
source URL, a link to the license, and the attribution line written out.

**The attribution line is where the points are.** An image with an empty license field is worth
nothing. Four items done properly beats six done sloppily, so if you run out of time, hand in
what you have.

Full instructions are in the activity. I'll be walking around, and I'm going to ask the same
