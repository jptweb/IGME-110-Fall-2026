## II. Real or Fake?

We'll look at a few things and you vote: real, or generated?

Then, and this is the actual point, whoever voted has to say why. What convinced you it was
real? What tipped you off that it wasn't?

You'll do better on some of these than others. Where the room gets it wrong is the interesting
part.

**The takeaway:** confidence is not evidence, and the check takes about thirty seconds.

## IV. Three Ideas Each

Study Guide 2 asked you to come out with three candidate topics. That's what you brought. Some of
you got there with AI, some didn't. Doesn't matter here.

The goal for the next eight minutes is to get everything out loud before anyone starts judging. A
team of four should end up with twelve ideas written down.

One way to run it, if you want a structure:

1. Each person reads their three ideas out loud
2. Hold the reactions until everyone has gone
3. One person writes them all down

Step 2 is the part worth protecting. If people start reacting on the second idea, ideas ten
through twelve never get said. Past that, run it however your team likes. Some of you already have
a brainstorming method that works for you, and I'd rather you use it than follow mine.

---

## V. Cut It Down to One

Now you argue. Three questions that tend to settle it quickly:

- Is anyone actually excited about it?
- Could you find three real articles about it?
- Which one is the most fun to argue for?

**You are not stuck with the twelve.** If the conversation turns up something better, or a mashup
of two ideas nobody brought in on their own, take it. The list is there to get you started.

**You don't have to be certain today.** Friday's work plan is what locks the topic in. If your
team is stuck, wave me over. Being stuck at this stage is normal and there's a trick for it.

## VI. What Project 1 Actually Asks

A researched proposal for a product that doesn't exist yet: a phone app, a web app, a website, a
desktop program, or a physical thing.

**→ [Full instructions: Project 1, Product Proposal](../documents/p1-product-proposal.md)**

Some real ones from last year: *Excuse Genie*, *Taser Bed* (a bed that shocks you awake),
*Bike Spikes* (traction control for bicycles on winter roads), *BetWise* (an app for people with
a gambling addiction).

Weird is welcome. **The idea is not the hard part.** The hard part is convincing a skeptical
reader that someone should actually fund this.

### Everyone has a job

These are jobs, not chapters. Each one runs the whole three weeks, and none of them means writing
a section alone.

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| Coordinator | The schedule and the check-ins. Who's doing what by when. |
| Editor | How it reads. One voice, and the final pass. |
| Evidence Lead | Every claim has something behind it. |
| Product Lead | What the thing actually is, and keeping the concept from drifting. |

*(Team of three? Coordinator and Editor fold together. Team of five? Add a second Evidence Lead.)*

**Two rules on top of that.** Everyone writes sections, handed out week by week in your work plan.
And everyone makes at least one mockup, because Section 3 needs three and there are four of you.

**Nobody is assigned to be the skeptic.** Instead the Coordinator runs five minutes at the end of
every meeting: what's the weakest thing in here right now? Those notes become Section 4, which is
the part that separates a proposal from a sales pitch.

## VII. The Work Plan

Due **Friday**. Check myCourses for the exact time.

It asks for your product idea, who has which role, the ideas you turned down, and how your team
plans to reach each other. It's short. It exists because most group projects that go badly went
badly in the first week, when nobody said out loud who was doing what.

**One person submits it for the whole team**, and the submission is two links pasted into a text
box: your Work Plan doc and your proposal doc.

### Set sharing when you make the copies

Two settings on both docs, and then you're done thinking about it:

- **Your teammates: Editor**
- **General access: Anyone at RIT with the link, Viewer**

You don't need to share anything with me separately. I'm at RIT, so the link is enough. The second
setting is also what lets another team open your proposal in week 3 to critique it, so it isn't
optional.

**Not sure how to do either one?**
