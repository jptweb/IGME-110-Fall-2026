## II. From a Wall of Text to Five Elements

You already read infographics all day: a health bar, a minimap, your Spotify Wrapped. They work
because somebody decided what you should get at a glance, and that decision is what Project 2 is
really about.

We start with one page from a real Fall 2025 proposal: Grub Guru, an app that suggests recipes
from a photo of your fridge. It's solid work, and it's full of numbers, and nobody is ever going to
read it on a poster.

Turning it into an infographic takes three moves, and none of them are design yet:

1. **Find the numbers.** Every figure, every claim you could point at.
2. **Pick the story.** Numbers aren't a story until you decide what they're saying. "Nutrition apps
   are a market that doubles by 2030" is a story. "Some statistics about nutrition apps" isn't.
3. **Keep five.** The story tells you which five things earn a spot and which ones are noise.

Then we look at [the infographic a student actually made](https://github.com/tonethar/IGME-110-Fall-2025/blob/main/documents/_infographic-finals/04-Grub%20Guru-1.pdf)
from the same proposal. They took a completely different angle and didn't use a single number
from that page. The numbers they did use aren't in the proposal at all, because they went and
found them. **The angle decides everything else**, including what data you go looking for. That's
why it's the first thing your Plan asks for.

What it *looks* like comes later. The mood board is Tuesday, and what makes an infographic work is
week 7. Today is only about what goes on it.

## IV. Studio: Pick Your Proposal

Most of today is yours. By the end of class you should have:

1. **A proposal picked, and a backup.** Read a few before you decide. You're looking for one with
   enough behind it that you have something to show.
2. **Your angle in one sentence.** Something like *"Grub Guru: nutrition apps are a market that
   doubles by 2030."* If your sentence is just the product's name, keep going.
3. **A start on the first three sections of your Plan.**
4. **Two or three infographics you like, saved.** Last year's finals count. Your Plan asks for a
   couple of reference infographics, and Tuesday's mood board goes faster if you already have some.

I'll be coming around asking one question: **what's your angle?**
