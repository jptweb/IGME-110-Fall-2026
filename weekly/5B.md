## II. From a Wall of Text to Five Elements

We start with one paragraph from a real Fall 2025 proposal: Grub Guru, an app that suggests
recipes from a photo of your fridge. It's a solid paragraph, and it's full of numbers, and nobody
is ever going to read it on a poster.

Turning it into an infographic takes three moves, and none of them are design yet:

1. **Find the numbers.** Every figure, every claim you could point at.
2. **Pick the story.** Numbers aren't a story until you decide what they're saying. "Nutrition apps
   are a market that doubles by 2030" is a story. "Some statistics about nutrition apps" isn't.
3. **Keep five.** The story tells you which five things earn a spot and which ones are noise.

Then we look at [the infographic a student actually made](https://github.com/tonethar/IGME-110-Fall-2025/blob/main/documents/_infographic-finals/04-Grub%20Guru-1.pdf)
from the same proposal. They took a completely different angle and didn't use a single number
from that paragraph. It's still a good infographic. **The angle decides everything else**, which
is why it's the first thing your Plan asks for.

One more thing to notice: if your angle needs data the proposal doesn't have, finding it is part
of the job.

What it *looks* like comes later. The mood board is Tuesday, and what makes an infographic work is
week 7. Today is only about what goes on it.
