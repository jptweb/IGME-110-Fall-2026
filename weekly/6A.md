## II. What a Mood Board Is

A mood board is one page that shows what a design is going to *feel* like before you design it: a
color palette, a couple of typefaces, and a handful of images, all picked to say the same thing.

In class we'll look at two boards that use the exact same layout and the exact same placeholder
text, and feel nothing alike. That's the whole idea. The layout didn't change. The colors, the
type, and the images did.

## III. CRAP: Four Rules That Don't Expire

The graphic design course covered space and grids. These four rules are how you actually arrange
things, and they apply to a web page, a slide, a poster, or a mood board.

- **Contrast:** if two things are different, make them *look* different. Important things should
  stand out.
- **Repetition:** reuse the same colors, fonts, and styles so the whole thing hangs together.
- **Alignment:** line things up. Nothing should be placed at random.
- **Proximity:** put related things close together and unrelated things apart.

The example we walk through in class is a web page older than most of you, and the lesson still
holds. For a current one, look at
[this trajectory graphic from Starship's Flight 14](https://sxcontent9668.azureedge.us/cms-assets/assets/Test_Flight_14_Trajectory_090226_web_2_56b3b513b2.png):
a whole mission in one picture, with every one of the four rules in it and not a single number.
Deciding what to leave out is the hard part, and it's yours to do.

You'll go much deeper on this in 235.

## IV. Mood Board

**[→ Activity: Mood Board](../activities/mood-board.md)** · 15 points · hand in by the end of the day

The short version:

1. Say who your infographic is for.
2. Pick 3 to 5 adjectives for how it should feel to them.
3. Build a palette with hex codes, collect at least four images, and choose two typefaces.
4. Arrange it using CRAP.

**Hand in however far you got.** It doesn't need to be finished. A real start gets full credit, so
if you're not sure whether you've done enough, raise your hand and ask.

**This is version one.** Last year this was a practice board on a made-up topic, and everyone had
to make a second one for the Plan. This year the board you start today *is* the one for your Plan,
so it counts twice. It's also supposed to change before Friday, so don't treat anything on it as
final.

**Haven't picked a proposal?** Pick one from the [Project 1 teams page](../documents/p1-teams.md),
or raise your hand and I'll give you a topic.

At the end of class you'll swap with a neighbor and guess each other's adjectives from the board
alone. If your neighbor gets close, the board is working.
