## II. The Chart That Lied

In 2014 Reuters published a chart of gun deaths in Florida. Most people who saw it read it as deaths
dropping sharply after 2005. They actually went up. The y-axis was upside down, with zero at the top.

The designer said that was a style choice, borrowed from another graphic that also flips its axis.
That one works, because its shape (blood running down a wall) tells you it's upside down before you
read a single number. The Florida chart copied the trick and left out the cue.

The point: every design choice is an argument, including the ones you make by accident. You won't
be standing next to your infographic when somebody reads it.

The whole story, if you want it:
[Misleading Gun-Death Chart Draws Fire](https://www.livescience.com/45083-misleading-gun-death-chart.html)
(LiveScience).

## III. Three Questions for Any Design

*"Good designers can explain every design choice from the goal."* The goal is just what you want
someone to think, feel, or do when they see your work.

These three questions get you there. They work on anything: an infographic, a website, a game HUD,
a slide.

1. **Where does your eye go first?**
2. **Who is this for?**
3. **What's the one thing it wants you to remember?**

In class we ran them on a few infographics from last year's class. Now run them on your own draft.
If your eye lands first on something that isn't the point, that's your next fix.

They also line up with your Mockup documentation: who it's for, what it should accomplish, and why
you laid it out the way you did. Answer them now and Friday's writing is half done.

## IV. Lab Time: Rough but Whole

By Thursday your draft needs everything in
[what the infographic must contain](../documents/p2-infographic.md#what-the-infographic-must-contain),
even if it's rough:

- A title that makes the subject clear at a glance
- At least five elements, each one labeled
- An image for every element
- One look: color, type, and images that hold together (this is where your mood board comes in)

Grey boxes count. Get all five elements on the page before you polish any one of them.

Two things that cost people marks every year:

- **Leaving a template as it came.** Placeholder icons, colors, or dummy text still in there reads
  as unfinished.
- **Screenshotting charts out of your proposal.** Rebuild them so they match your colors.
