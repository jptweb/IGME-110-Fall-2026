## II. How This Works

Full instructions: **[Project 1, section 3: Critiques](../documents/p1-product-proposal.md#3-critiques)**

**[→ The peer critique questions](../documents/p1-peer-critique-questions.md)** ·
[copy the template from the Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1RG1E2jPlseNaTt3XVv8_Wbj7VNCaUiTdTKRZwAJ2amw/)

Eleven questions across the six proposal sections. Read them before class if you want, but you
don't have to. They're written to be answered cold.

For each team you review:

1. Create a Google Doc named `Feedback for team [#], from team [#], section [#]`
2. Set general access to **Anyone at RIT with the link, Viewer**. Same setting you used on your own
   proposal. If they can't open it, it didn't happen.
3. Copy the questions from the link above into it
4. Pick **one scribe** for your team

Then for each question: read the relevant part of their proposal, discuss it as a group, agree on
an answer, and the scribe types what you agreed.

> ⚠️ **Do not split the questions up between people.** This is the one rule I care about today.
> The value is the argument your team has on the way to an answer. Divide it up and you get six
> disconnected opinions instead of one useful review, and I can tell the difference immediately.

**Post each finished critique as a new message in [#110-inclass-activities](https://rit.enterprise.slack.com/messages/C0BRPBGM7H8) as you go**, so the team you
reviewed can start using it tonight instead of Friday.

Start the message with `Section [01 or 07] · Review of Team [their number]`, then the link. That
first line is how a team finds the review of their own proposal. **If you are the team being
reviewed, reply in that message's thread**, so the conversation stays attached to the critique
it's about.

## III. Writing the Critiques

One question to keep asking each other while you write: **"that's a nice sentence, what should
they do about it?"** If a point doesn't survive that question, it isn't finished yet.

I'll be around. Wave me over if you want a second opinion on something, otherwise I'll leave you
to it.

Three things that go wrong, so you can skip them:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Everything is positive** | You're allowed to say something is broken. They would much rather hear it from you today than from me in week 5, when it's worth 100 points. |
| **You split it up** | See above. Restart as a group; it doesn't fix itself. |
| **The draft you're assigned is empty** | Write that at the top of your critique, note when you tried, move on to the next one. **Your grade is not affected by the state of somebody else's draft.** Don't spend the period chasing them. |

**Finish one in class.** The second will probably spill past the period, and that's expected. Both
are due Friday night.

## IV. What You Get Back

By tonight there should be two critiques of your proposal sitting in Slack.

**Read them before you touch the document.** Some of it will be wrong. Some of it will sting and be
right. Some of it will be two teams independently pointing at the same section, which is the most
useful signal you'll get all project, because it means the problem is real and not a matter of
taste.

Next week is the final push. The proposal is due Friday of week 4 and there's limited class time
for it, so the revising happens on your schedule.
