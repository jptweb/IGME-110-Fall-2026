# Activity: Critique Rehearsal

**IGME-110 | Week 3A | In-Class**

> This one is not graded and there is nothing to hand in. It is the rehearsal for
> Thursday, when two teams read your proposal and you read theirs.

You read Grant Wiggins for the study guide, so the frame is already in your head: a
useful critique is **goal-referenced, actionable, and timely**. Today we use it on
somebody else's work before we use it on each other's.

## The questions

These are the same ones you'll use Thursday, cut down to what fits a single class
period. Full set: [Project 1 peer critique questions](../documents/p1-peer-critique-questions.md)

1. What is this product, in one sentence? If you can't answer from their document, that
   is the finding.
2. Who is it for, and does the research they cite actually describe those people?
3. Which three features are the core ones? Can you tell?
4. What claim in here is unsupported, and what would support it?
5. What's the thinnest section, and what specifically should they do about it?

## The rule

**"That's a nice sentence, what should they do about it?"**

Every observation gets a follow-up. "The market research is weak" is not feedback until
it becomes "you claim students want this, and your source is about people over 40."

The polite critique is the worthless one. Being vague isn't kind, it just moves the
problem to week 4 when it's expensive.
