# Activity: Mood Board

**IGME-110 | Week 6A | In-Class Activity | 15 points**

A mood board is one page that shows what a design is going to *feel* like before you design it.
It's a collage: a color palette, a couple of typefaces, and a handful of images, all picked to say
the same thing. Designers make one at the start of almost every visual project, because "modern but
friendly" means something different to everyone until you can point at it.

Today you start the one for your Project 2 infographic.

Want to read more? [Mood Boards in UX: How and Why to Use Them](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mood-boards/)
from Nielsen Norman Group covers the same process: pick a few mood words, collect visuals, then
arrange them.

**The short version:** start a mood board for the proposal you picked, and hand in however far you
got. It doesn't need to be finished.

## This is version one

Last year this was a practice board on a made-up topic, and then everyone had to make a second,
real one for the Project 2 Plan a few days later. This year you skip the practice round. The board
you start today is the one that ends up in your Plan, so the work counts twice.

The catch: **today's board is a first draft.** It should change before Friday, and it will
probably change again when you build your mockup. Nothing you pick today is locked in.

## Haven't picked a proposal yet?

That's OK. Open the [Project 1 teams page](../documents/p1-teams.md), skim a few, and pick one.
Any team's proposal is fair game, and it doesn't have to be your own.

Still stuck? Raise your hand and I'll give you a topic to work with.

If you end up switching proposals later, there's no guarantee today's board carries over, and
that's fine. Starting a visual project with a mood board is a good habit either way.

## How to build it

### 1. Say who it's for

Your Plan asks for an audience, so start there. One line is enough: *"first-year students cooking
for themselves for the first time"* is a person you can design for. "Everyone" isn't.

### 2. Pick 3 to 5 adjectives

How should your infographic feel to that person? *Playful, bright, friendly* makes a very different
board from *calm, trustworthy, clean*. Every other choice you make today gets checked against these
words, so spend a couple of minutes on them.

### 3. Build a color palette

Four to six colors, with the hex code for each one. [Adobe Color](https://color.adobe.com/),
[Coolors](https://coolors.co/), and [Colormind](http://colormind.io/) all work. You made one of
these for Study Guide 6, so you already know the drill.

A shortcut: find a photo that already feels right, and Adobe Color or Colormind will pull a palette
out of it.

### 4. Collect images

At least four: photos, illustrations, textures, and icons in the style you'd actually use.
Infographics lean hard on icons, so it's worth deciding now whether yours are flat, outlined,
hand-drawn, or something else.

If you picked your palette first, you can
[search for images by color](https://htmlcolorcodes.com/blog/how-to-search-for-images-by-color/).
Keep a note of where each image came from. You'll need to cite them in the project anyway.

### 5. Choose two typefaces

One for headlines, one for body text. Type out a sample of each at a couple of sizes and weights,
and label each one with the font's name. [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/) has a "Feeling"
filter that's handy here, and the font-pairing reading from Study Guide 6 applies directly.

### 6. Arrange it

Don't just dump everything on a page. How the board is laid out says something too. This is where
CRAP comes in:

- **Contrast:** make the important things stand out, like your adjectives and your palette.
- **Repetition:** reuse your own colors and fonts for the board's labels and headings.
- **Alignment:** line things up on a grid instead of scattering them.
- **Proximity:** keep related things together, so colors sit with colors and type samples sit with
  type samples.

Put your proposal, your audience, and your adjectives on the board itself. If you've already saved
an infographic or two that you like, drop them in as well. They're optional today and required in
the Plan.

## Tools

Use whatever you're fastest in.

- **Canva** is the easiest. Search the templates for "mood board," then replace everything in it
  with your own stuff.
- **Adobe Express** works the same way.
- **PowerPoint** works better than you'd think. Make one slide your board. Everything is easy to
  drag around, which makes it a good place to try out CRAP. Google Slides works too.
- **Figma** and **Photoshop** are both on the machines in GOL 2000.

## Guess my adjectives

In the last few minutes of class, swap with someone next to you. Look at their board for 30
seconds, without them telling you anything, and write down the three adjectives you think it's
going for. Then compare.

If they got close, your board is working. If they didn't, that's the first thing to fix before
Friday.

## What to hand in

**Hand in however far you got.** It doesn't need to be done, and I'm not grading it like a
finished piece. This one is mostly pass/fail: a real start gets full credit. If what you hand in is
clearly thrown together just to have something to submit, I may take points off. Not sure if what
you have is enough? Raise your hand in class and ask.

On myCourses, by the end of the day:

1. **Your board as it stands**, as a PNG or JPG. An export or a screenshot is fine.
2. **Three short lines:**
   - Who it's for, and your adjectives
   - What your partner guessed
   - What you'll add or change before Friday

That's it. The finished board goes into your
[Project 2 Plan](../documents/p2-infographic.md#1-plan) on Friday, and that's where it gets graded
properly.

## AI Expectations

Same rules as [Project 2](../documents/p2-infographic.md#ai-expectations). Generating images or
icons for the board is fine. The choices are yours to make: the adjectives, the palette, the type,
and how it all fits together. Don't ask a tool to build the whole board for you.

If you use AI for anything, add a line to your hand-in saying which tool and what you used it for.
