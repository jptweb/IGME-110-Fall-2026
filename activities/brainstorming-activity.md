# Activity: Brainstorming a Product Idea

**IGME-110 | Week 1B | In-Class Activity | 10 points**

## What this is for

Take a real problem and develop it into a product idea, using a thinking partner to get there.
**Most of you will use AI as that partner. There's a version below that uses a classmate
instead, and it earns exactly the same credit.**

We're doing it today so the process is familiar next week, when you'll run it with your
Project 1 group.

The goal is not "get an idea handed to you." The goal is to notice the difference between a
partner that thinks *with* you and one that hands you something generic.

## Pick one problem

- **Doomscrolling.** You can't stop scrolling.
- **Brain rot.** Mentally fried from too much screen time.
- **Group project scheduling.** Five people, five schedules, no meeting time.

*I may adjust this list before Thursday. If I do, I'll say so at the start of class, and this page
will match what we actually run.*

## Prefer not to use AI? Read this.

There's a version of this activity that uses no AI at all, and it earns the same credit.
No explanation needed and no penalty. Just tell me you're taking it.

**The partner version:** pair up with a classmate and take turns. One of you asks the
questions from Parts 1 and 2 below, the other answers out loud. Then swap. You'll produce the
same thing: a specific problem, a list of possible solutions, and one idea developed far
enough to describe. Sketch your visual by hand instead of generating it.

Everything else, including the Slack post, works the same way.

*(Worth knowing: the partner version is closer to how this actually happens in industry. The
AI version is faster, not better.)*

## Part 1: Understand the problem (7 minutes)

### Step 1: Start the conversation

Paste this, filling in your choice:

```
I want to design a solution for [doomscrolling / brain rot / group project scheduling].
Help me understand this problem better by asking me questions about my experience with it.
```

### Step 2: Actually engage

- Answer honestly about **your** experience, not a generic student's
- Let it ask you three or four follow-up questions
- Notice that it's making you think, rather than thinking for you
- Save anything it helps you realize

If Guided Learning isn't giving you what you want, you can turn it off with the X.

## Part 2: Generate solutions (8 minutes)

### Step 3: Brainstorm wide

```
Based on our discussion, give me 10 creative solutions including:
- 3 practical mobile app ideas
- 3 completely ridiculous but funny ideas
- 2 physical products
- 2 social or service-based solutions

After showing me the list, help me pick the most interesting one to develop further.
```

### Step 4: Develop your favorite

Pick the one that makes you laugh or makes you think "that could actually work." Tell Gemini
which one, answer its follow-ups, add your own twist, and give it a name.

### Step 5: Get specific

```
My solution is called [YOUR NAME FOR IT].
Help me define:
- Who EXACTLY would use this? (be specific, not just "students")
- What's the #1 feature that would make it actually helpful?
- What would make it fun to use daily?
```

## Part 4: Share it (5 minutes)

### Step 8: Post to Slack

Go to **[#110-inclass-activities](https://rit.enterprise.slack.com/messages/C0BRPBGM7H8)** in the class Slack and post a **new message** with:

separates using AI from trusting it.

## Tips

**Don't take the first suggestion.** Push back, ask for variations, add your own ideas.

**Be specific.** "Tired students" is nothing. "Night owl gamers who stay up until 4am" is
something.

**Have fun with it.** The stupid ideas usually lead somewhere better than the sensible ones.

**Save your chat.** You'll want it for the week 2 study guide.
