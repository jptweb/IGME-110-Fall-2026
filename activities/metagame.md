# Activity: The Metagame

**IGME-110 | Week 3A | In-Class**

> This one is not graded and there is nothing to hand in. If your team would rather spend
> the time on your draft, do that instead. It's due tomorrow night.

You read about what makes a critique useful for the study guide. This is that idea with no
stakes attached: you argue about video games for fifteen minutes, and somebody has to decide
who argued it better and say why.

That last part is the whole reason we're playing.

## What's in the deck

**White cards are video games.** Minecraft, Oregon Trail, Dark Souls, Candyland, Twister.

**Purple cards are questions.** "Which is most magical?" "Which will outlive us all?" "The
thing that everyone loves to hate."

**A few purple cards are blank, and that's on purpose.** If one turns up when you're setting up,
your Judge writes the question. Anything works as long as people can argue about it.

## How to play

The full rules are on the sheet at your table. The short version:

One person is the Judge. They flip a purple question. Everybody else plays the white card
that best answers it, then gets 45 seconds to argue for it. The Judge picks the best argument
and the worst one, and has to explain both choices out loud.

Two rounds, then stop. There is no prize.

## The part that matters

**The Judge is not picking the better game. The Judge is picking the better argument.**

Those two come apart faster than you'd think. Somebody will play a card nobody would defend
and win the round anyway, because they made a real case for it. Somebody else will play the
obvious answer and lose, because "it's just better" is not a reason.

That gap is the same one the study guide was about, and it's what you're being asked for on
Thursday. Not "I liked it" or "I didn't." A claim, with something holding it up.

## Thursday

Two teams open your proposal draft and tell you what's wrong with it, and you do the same to
theirs. The Judge's job today, saying out loud why one argument beat another, is that job
with lower stakes and worse cards.
