# Activity: Paper Prototype of a Digital Game

**IGME-110 | Week 9A | In-Class Activity**

> 🚧 **Draft.** Adapted from the Fall 2025 version. Submission method is being reworked.

Before you build anything on a screen, you build it out of paper and whatever else is on
the table. It's faster, it's cheaper, and it's much easier to throw away, which is the
whole point.

Today you do that with a game.

## What you'll do

**Get into groups of about four.** Your P1 team, your P3 team, or whoever you're sitting
near, it doesn't matter.

**Spin for a game.** You'll get one assigned rather than picking a favorite, because the
constraint is part of the exercise.

**Find the core experience.** Not every mechanic, not the whole game. The one thing a
player is actually there for. That's what your prototype has to deliver.

**Build it.** There's paper, cards, dice, and assorted junk on the back table. Use a
pencil and whatever else you have.

**Then answer four questions about it:**

1. What's the **time scale**: slow, real-time, fast?
2. What **actions** can a player take? What are they actually choosing between?
3. What **emotions** do you want them to feel?
4. What's one **emergent** thing that happens when people play it, that you didn't design
   directly?

## Two frames worth having

**MDA: Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics.** You design mechanics. Players experience
aesthetics. Dynamics are what happens in between, and you can't control them directly.
Question 4 is asking you to notice one.

**The eight kinds of fun.** "Fun" is not one thing. Sensation, fantasy, narrative,
challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression, and submission are all different, and a game
that's great at one may be dull at another. Knowing which one you're aiming at makes the
core experience much easier to name.

**Using AI to brainstorm is fine and encouraged here**, as long as you say so. It's good at
throwing out twenty mechanics fast. It is not good at knowing which one your table will
actually enjoy, and you'll usually want something simpler and more focused than what it
gives you.

## Next class

Each team gets about two minutes to put their prototype up and walk the room through it.
Photos are fine, you don't need to bring the physical thing back.
