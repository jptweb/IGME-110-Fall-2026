## What you're making

One video, about 50 seconds long, with these parts:

| Part | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Title card** | Your name and a title, on screen for 5 seconds |
| **Picture for the whole narration** | Something on screen from the first word to the last, and it ends when the narration ends |
| **Cuts** | At least 5 changes from one picture to the next. At least 2 of those 5 are transition effects (a dissolve, a wipe) instead of plain cuts |
| **Sources** | At least 3 different pieces of visual material, in any mix of video clips and still images. Any still image has to move (pan or zoom) |
| **Music** | Background music under the narration, quiet enough that the voice is easy to hear |
| **Credits** | 10 seconds at the end, listing where every clip, image, and song came from (the site and the creator or title is enough) |

A few things people ask about that table:

- **What counts as a source?** One clip or one image. Three clips from the same website are
  three sources. The title card, the credits, and the music don't count toward the three.
- **Does it have to be video?** No. Three stills that pan and zoom meet the requirement, and so
  do three video clips, or a mix. Try to use at least one real video clip, since Project 4
  will need them.
- **How do I count cuts?** Every time the picture changes, that's one, including going from
  the title card into the first shot and from the last shot into the credits. Those two spots
  are the easy places for your two transitions.
- **Three sources but five cuts?** Right. Title, three shots, and credits only makes four
  changes, so you'll come back to one of your sources or add a fourth.
- **How long is it?** A 5 second title during the quiet at the start, pictures until the
  narration ends at about 0:40, then 10 seconds of credits.

You find or shoot the visuals. They don't have to match the story literally. A dragon can be a
cat. A castle can be Golisano Hall. Funny is allowed and usually works better.

Stick to Creative Commons, public domain, or AI-generated material, or footage you shoot
yourself. Places that are free and don't make you sign up or pay:

| For | Where |
|---|---|
| **Stock video** | [Pexels Videos](https://www.pexels.com/videos/) (search works well), [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-video/), [Coverr](https://coverr.co/). [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/) is fine too, but skip the sponsored row at the top of its results, which is paid |
| **Old and strange footage** | The [Prelinger Archives](https://archive.org/details/prelinger): thousands of public domain films from the 1930s to 1960s. Very good for comedy |
| **Space and science** | [NASA's image and video library](https://images.nasa.gov/), all public domain |
| **Anything specific** | [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Videos). Clunky, but no ads and every file lists its license |
| **Game footage** | Record your own. Most games let you capture your own play (Windows: Win+G, Mac: Cmd+Shift+5, or OBS on either). Don't download someone else's gameplay video |
| **Music** | [YouTube Audio Library](https://www.youtube.com/audiolibrary), [Incompetech](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/music.html), [Free Music Archive](https://freemusicarchive.org/) |

[This Harvard guide](https://guides.library.harvard.edu/c.php?g=310751) lists more. Whatever
you use, note where it came from as you download it, because that list becomes your credits.

## What you can do without sound

We start this in class, and a lab with no headphones is a bad place to edit audio. That's
fine, because about half of this assignment needs no sound at all. Do these first:

1. **Install your editor** and make the project folder.
2. **Fill in a shot list.** Copy the [cue sheet](#the-cue-sheet) below and write what's on
   screen for each line. This is a small version of the storyboard in your Project 4 Work Plan.
3. **Collect your sources.** At least three, saved into the project folder. Write down where
   each one came from now, because that list is your credits.
4. **Build the title card and the credits.**
5. **Put the narration on the timeline and rough in your clips** against the times on the cue
   sheet. You can see where each line starts in the waveform, the picture of the sound that
   the editor draws on the track.

Then at home, with sound: check each cut against the voice, add the music and set its level,
and export.

## The cue sheet

Times are from the start of the file and are close, not exact. The track opens with about 6
seconds of quiet before the first word, which is where your 5 second title card goes.

| Time | The line starts with | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | *(quiet)* | Title card |
| 0:06 | "Once upon a time..." | |
| 0:09 | "But she had an enchantment..." | |
| 0:13 | "which could only be broken..." | |
| 0:16 | "She was locked away..." | |
| 0:18 | "guarded by a terrible..." | |
| 0:22 | "Many brave knights..." | |
| 0:28 | "She waited in the dragon's keep..." | |
| 0:33 | "for her true love..." | |
| 0:37 | "Like that's ever gonna happen." | |
| 0:40 | *(narration ends)* | Credits, 10 seconds |

Nine lines and five required cuts, so you don't need a new picture on every line.

## First steps in Shotcut

[Shotcut](https://shotcut.org/download/) is free, runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux, and is what
I show in class. If you already know iMovie, CapCut, Clipchamp, DaVinci Resolve, or Premiere,
use that instead. The requirements are the same in any editor.

The tutorial above covers all of this at a slower pace. These are the same steps as a checklist
to follow once you have watched it:

1. **Start a project.** Open Shotcut, pick your project folder, give the project a name, and
   press Start. Save right away.
2. **Bring files in.** Drag your clips, images, and `narration.mp3` into the **Playlist**
   panel. The playlist is your pile of ingredients. Nothing is in the video yet.
3. **Put a clip on the timeline.** Drag one from the Playlist down to the **Timeline**. That
   makes the first video track.
4. **Add an audio track** from the timeline's menu (Track Operations, Add Audio Track), and
   drag the narration onto it. You should see its waveform.
5. **Trim** by dragging either end of a clip. **Split** by putting the playhead where you want
   the cut and pressing `S`. Select a piece you don't want and delete it.
6. **Make a transition** by dragging a clip sideways so it overlaps its neighbor on the same
   track. The overlap becomes a dissolve.
7. **Add text.** This is the part that trips people up. Use **File, Open Other, Text** to make
   a text clip, type your title, then drag it to the timeline like any other clip.
8. **Pan and zoom on a still.** Select the image on the timeline, open **Filters**, and add
   **Size, Position & Rotate**. Turn on keyframes, set the starting size and position at the
   start of the clip, then move to the end and set where it should finish.
9. **Export.** Open the **Export** panel, keep the default preset (it makes an H.264 `.mp4`),
   press Export File, and wait for the job to finish before you close Shotcut.

Stuck on one step? Go back to that part of the tutorial, or search "Shotcut" plus what you're
trying to do.

## Finishing at home

- **Watch the tutorial first** if you haven't yet. Everything below assumes you have.
- **Check your cuts against the voice.** A cut that lands half a second late looks like a
  mistake. A cut on the first word of a line looks deliberate.
- **Add the music on its own audio track** and turn it down. In Shotcut, select the music
  clip, open Filters, and add **Gain / Volume**. Start around -18 dB and adjust by ear. If you
  have to strain to hear the narration, it's still too loud.
- **Watch it once from the top without touching anything.** Then fix the one thing that
  bothered you most.

## AI Expectations

Same rules as Project 4, because this is practice for it.

- **Fine:** AI-generated music, images, or short clips used as source material, credited like
  any other source. Asking an AI how to do something in your editor.
- **Not fine:** an AI tool or an auto-edit feature that assembles the video for you. The cuts,
  the timing, and the audio level are the assignment.
